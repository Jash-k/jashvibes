The ReTro page, eight ways. Desktop and mobile side by side.

Every mock keeps the shell you already chose — the left rail on desktop/TV, the dock on phones — and repaints only what lives inside /classics. Titles, ratings and counts are sample data for layout only. The real contract is what the page has today: /api/vod giving title, year, rating, voteCount, synopsis, posterUrl, backdropUrl, genres[], sources[], streamsCount, tmdbMatched, plus a paged grid, filters, and the first-load sync when Mongo is empty. Where a direction needs something extra, its notes say so in amber; nothing is promised that the API cannot answer.

Shared by all 8

Rail + dock, one sync affordance, Load-more paging, and the day-mode rule from the last fix: artwork keeps its own light in both themes, so a poster wall never washes out.

Deleted by all 8

The ← Home pill, the sticky bar around it, the BrandLogo strip, the 6-field filter slab in one bordered box, and the max-w-7xl column that ignores the rail.

TV is a first-class screen

No hover-only controls, focus rings on cards and rows, and nothing that needs a drag. Lean-back ideas are marked in each note.

Free-tier budget

One paged request per view. Anything needing counts, decades or aggregation is flagged, because the endpoint does not compute them yet.

1

Marquee Wall — the theatre front, then the whole shelf

A dot-matrix ticker carries the facts a hero wastes on one title (count, span, source), then an eight-wide wall where the two headliners double-size. Dense, loud, and instantly readable from across a room — the most "cinema hall" of the eight.

  • Best forShowing off the size of the collection; lean-back TV at 10 feet.
  • DataAlready there: title, year, rating, poster, sources. The ticker's "412 titles · 1952–2009" is total + facets.minYear/maxYear, both already returned.
  • NeedsNothing new. Headliners = page 1 sorted by rating, so it is a CSS treatment of the same fetch.
  • MobileTicker collapses to one line, wall becomes 2-up, headliners go full-width.
  • RiskDense walls make unreadable titles below 40 px on 720p TVs — needs a size floor.
2

Contact Sheet — one strip per genre, frames numbered

The page becomes what a film lab hands you: sprocket holes, frame numbers, one strip per genre you walk with arrow keys or a thumb. Nothing is hidden behind pagination — the strip is the browse, and the numbers make a 400-title catalogue feel archival instead of searchable.

  • Best forFast horizontal scanning; the most distinct "archive" identity of the eight.
  • DataAlready there: genres[] from facets, one strip per genre. Frame numbers are the row's index — display only.
  • NeedsOne decision: strips need one request per genre (cheap, cached) or a single bigger page split client-side. I would fetch limit=60 once and bucket it in the browser — zero extra load.
  • TVBest here: focus walks the strip, scroll follows the focused frame; no dragging needed.
  • RiskSquare thumbs are a crop of a 2:3 poster; some titles will look chopped without a poster-object-position tune.
3

The Decade Room — year is the whole navigation BUILT IN v8.10.0

Delete the grid-first idea. You choose a decade on a ruler, and the page shows that decade in release order down a spine — a timeline, not a shelf. Try the ruler in the desktop mock: it is the interaction that sells it.

  • Best forHow you actually pick a classic — "give me something from the 80s". Replaces the year-from/year-to inputs and the decade chips with one control.
  • DataAlready there: yearFrom/yearTo are live query params today; the ruler just writes them.
  • NeedsOptional: the counts on the ruler (96 · 118 · 133) want a decade $bucket in the facets aggregation — one extra stage, run on the same query, no new request. Without it, the ruler shows no numbers.
  • MobileRuler becomes a snap-scroll segmented control; the spine keeps the year in the left gutter.
  • RiskA decade with 3 titles looks empty — needs a "also from nearby years" tail.
4

The Index — no posters, all titles

A ranking ledger: rank, title in a bookish serif, year, a rating bar, source, and how many playable streams. Zero images on first paint, so it is the fastest of the eight on a sleeping free-tier box and on a TV browser, and it reads like a filmography rather than a shop window.

  • Best forCoverage you can read: 60 titles on one screen instead of 24. Also the cheapest — no poster requests at all.
  • DataAlready there: every column exists on publicItem. voteCount turns "8.3" into "8.3 from 21k", which is the honesty the rating needs.
  • NeedsSmall: limit is capped at 60 server-side, so "Load more" walks 60 at a time. Fine.
  • TVExcellent: rows are keyboard-native, and 10-foot text is the point.
  • RiskDeliberately un-glamous. If you want the wall to look like cinema, this is the wrong pick — it is a library, not a poster.
5

Ticket Stubs — a collection you keep, not a shelf you scan

Each title is an admit-one stub with a serial, a seat, and a perforation; the page is a coupon book and the button is "add to reel". It is the only idea that treats nostalgia as the interface, and it is the one that looks its best in day mode — press the toggle at the top.

  • Best forMaking the page feel like yours; "I already watched that" is visible as a torn stub.
  • DataAlready there: favorite / history keys work for retro (retro:<id>), and lastSyncedAt is a real "added on" line.
  • NeedsOne thing: the "serial" is the Mongo _id tail — display only, invented by layout, never a claim. "Seat" would be your history rank, so a never-watched stub says SEAT — rather than lying.
  • MobileSingle column, thumb-friendly 44 px+ targets, the reel count pinned above the dock.
  • RiskMost decoration per pixel of the eight; paper texture has to be CSS gradients, not images, and low-detail stubs are slower to scan than a poster.
6

Auditorium — one backdrop, then the programme

Same grammar as your homepage, tuned for the archive: a single featured classic on its backdrop, and below it three lanes by genre plus a numbered "tonight's programme" column that is really a curated queue. Continuity is the feature — ReTro stops being a different app.

  • Best forPeople who land, watch the featured one, and want "what next" answered; consistent with RailFocus.
  • DataAlready there: backdropUrl is on the row, genres[] gives the lanes, synopsis gives the hero's one line.
  • NeedsHonest part: "tonight's programme" can't be a real curation service — no cron, no queue. It would be your history + top-rated-unwatched, computed client-side from the page you already fetched. I'd build it as "Start here" and label it that way.
  • ArtworkBackdrop is an artwork surface: dark in day mode too, per the rule the last fix taught us.
  • RiskOne hero = one title seen per visit; the archive's whole value is breadth. Lanes below must be wide enough to matter.
7

Reel Bin — a filter machine with a results window

For a catalogue you actually mine: a permanent facet column with live counts, wide 16:9 tiles (the backdrop, not the poster), a rating bar per row, saved-search chips, and search that is one field not a slab. This is the only idea where "show me every 80s Aha drama over 8.0" is one click instead of four inputs.

  • Best forYou, on a desktop, knowing what you want; also the best use of a wide window next to the rail.
  • DataAlready there: q, sort, source, genre, yearFrom, yearTo, minRating are all real query params today — the column is a re-skin of what the endpoint answers.
  • NeedsTwo things: (a) facet counts need one $group added to the existing aggregation — same request, no new one; (b) saved searches would be localStorage, like your other prefs — no DB writes. Backdrop per row means /api/vod must keep returning backdropUrl in list shape (it does) and that is 60 extra image requests, so on slow nets I'd show it only on hover/focus.
  • MobileThe column becomes a bottom sheet with an active-filter count, exactly as the mobile mock shows.
  • RiskThe most UI of the eight; on a TV it needs the facet column to be reachable by arrow keys or it should auto-hide.
8

Grain & Glow — the poster wall, nothing else

Chrome deleted until only the art is left: a six-column waterfall of real posters under a film-grain wash, one floating era pill, titles appearing only on hover or focus. The most beautiful of the eight on a big screen, and the one that treats the archive as a gallery.

  • Best forBrowsing without a goal; screenshots, TVs, and the "wow" first impression.
  • DataAlready there: posterUrl and the 2:3 box you already ship. Masonry is columns — one line of CSS.
  • NeedsReal cost: 60 posters is 60 requests on first paint. On a sleeping free tier that is the whole page budget — so it must lazy-load hard and hold the session cache you already write (jash:classics:v1). No counts, no facets, nothing in the background.
  • TV / keyboardHover-only captions are a problem on a remote: I'd reveal on :focus-within too (shown here) and keep the pill arrow-key reachable.
  • RiskUnmatched titles have no poster (tmdbMatched:false) and a wall shows that gap loudly — needs the drawn-title placeholder the current card already has, or the wall looks broken.
